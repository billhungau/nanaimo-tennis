# Nanaimo Tennis Futures

Build a polished, credible civic-information website about the future of indoor tennis in Nanaimo, British Columbia.

The site should feel like an independent community information hub, not a political attack site, commercial tennis-club website, or protest campaign.

The primary purpose is to:

Explain the City of Nanaimo's purchase of the Westwood Lake Tennis Club property.

Explain that the tennis club is scheduled to close and the indoor tennis bubble is expected to be removed.

Document all primary sources, news coverage, City documents, and public statements related to the issue.

Explain the role the facility has played in year-round recreation, junior tennis, lessons, tournaments, and City recreation programming.

Present a constructive community position:
"Pause removal of the indoor tennis facility until the City has completed public consultation and assessed feasible options for retaining year-round indoor tennis."

Track the positions of all Nanaimo mayoral and council candidates using the same questions for every candidate.

Allow residents to understand the issue and form their own opinion.

IMPORTANT TONE

The site must be factual, calm, respectful and evidence-based.

Do not make it look partisan.

Do not attack the City of Nanaimo, current councillors, staff, candidates, or the property seller.

Do not suggest corruption, secrecy or wrongdoing unless a sourced document explicitly establishes it.

Do not describe candidates as "pro-tennis" or "anti-tennis."

Do not rank candidates, endorse candidates, or tell users how to vote.

Candidate responses should be presented verbatim and equally.

The site should distinguish between:

A. the City's acquisition of the land; and
B. the separate decision to remove the indoor tennis bubble.

The community's principal request is not to reverse the land acquisition.

The principal request is:

"Before an existing year-round recreation facility is removed, we are asking the City to pause, consult the community, and evaluate whether there is a practical way to retain indoor tennis."

SECTION: THE QUESTION

Large editorial heading:

"The question is not whether the City should own the land."

Below:

"The City has acquired an important piece of land beside Westwood Lake Park. That decision can be considered separately from what happens to the recreation facilities already on the property.

The immediate question is simpler:

Should Nanaimo permanently remove an existing indoor recreation facility before completing public consultation and examining whether it could remain viable under another operating model?"

Present the final question in a highlighted quote-style card.

SECTION: WHAT WE ARE ASKING FOR

Heading:
"A pause, not a permanent commitment."

Text:

"We are not asking the City to commit today to operating a municipal tennis club.

We are asking Council to defer removal of the indoor tennis bubble until the City has:

• assessed the physical condition and remaining useful life of the facility;
• reviewed operating and maintenance costs;
• measured community demand for year-round indoor tennis;
• considered nonprofit, lease, partnership and community-operated models;
• completed meaningful public consultation.

Once that information is available, Council and the community can make a better-informed decision."

Create four small cards below:
Assess
Consult
Explore Options
Decide

SECTION: WHY THIS MATTERS

Create a restrained card grid.

Card 1:
"Year-round recreation"
Indoor tennis provides recreational access during Nanaimo's wet winter months.

Card 2:
"Junior development"
The facility has supported children's instruction and tennis development.

Card 3:
"Community programming"
Programs at the facility have included recreational lessons and activities accessible beyond club membership.

Card 4:
"An existing asset"
Once removed, rebuilding comparable indoor tennis infrastructure would require a new site, capital investment and planning.

Card 5:
"Growing community"
Nanaimo continues to grow, increasing long-term demand for recreation infrastructure.

Card 6:
"An irreversible decision"
A temporary pause preserves future options. Removing the facility eliminates one of them.

Do not exaggerate any claims. Every quantitative claim should eventually have a source.

SECTION: FAQ

Questions:

"Are you asking the City to reverse the property purchase?"

Answer:
"No. The land purchase and the future of the existing indoor tennis facility are separate questions. Our request is focused on preserving the option of year-round indoor tennis while the City's planning process takes place."

"Are you asking taxpayers to subsidize a private tennis club?"

Answer:
"No specific operating model is being proposed at this stage. We are asking the City to evaluate alternatives including nonprofit, lease and partnership models before removing the facility."

"Why can't players simply use outdoor courts?"

Answer:
"Outdoor courts remain valuable, but they do not provide dependable year-round access during Nanaimo's wet fall and winter months."

"Why act now?"

Answer:
"Once the indoor structure is removed, preserving the existing facility is no longer an option. A temporary pause allows the City to gather information before making an irreversible decision."

"Does this website oppose expansion of Westwood Lake Park?"

Answer:
"No. Expanding public parkland and evaluating whether an existing recreation facility can continue are not mutually exclusive."

DESIGN DETAILS

Use subtle scroll animations only.

No bouncing buttons.

No spinning tennis-ball animations.

Use tennis imagery sparingly.

Where appropriate, use documentary-style photographs:

indoor tennis courts

junior tennis

recreational adult tennis

Westwood Lake / Nanaimo landscape

Avoid stock photos showing professional ATP/WTA players.

The people depicted should look like ordinary community recreational players.

Use iconography only where it improves comprehension.

Candidate page should look closer to a voter-information guide than a political campaign page.

SOURCE INTEGRITY

Every significant factual claim should eventually be traceable to an original source.

Display:
"Last updated: [date]"

Where sources disagree, state that clearly.

Where information is unknown, do not speculate.

OVERALL MESSAGE

The website should leave visitors with this impression:

"The land purchase may have benefits for Nanaimo. Before removing an existing indoor recreation facility, however, it is reasonable to understand its condition, value, demand and possible operating alternatives. A short pause allows that assessment to happen."

The site should be persuasive through evidence, transparency and reasonableness rather than emotionally charged advocacy.
